The rep script (v6: the first dial, as it happens today)

Draft for ratification. The first dial to a form lead carries the whole conversation; voicemail, follow-up, returning a call, and inbound live are the other four. Form fields come from the ERP intake form on /apply-to-be-a-patient (no separately built lead form exists in the Williams site repos). The fact sheet is the only source of facts. Amber lines are the forks. Self-scheduling is a note at the bottom.

What the rep knows before dialing (from the form)

The form gives the rep: patient name · email · cellphone · country, state, city · language · referral source · "What is your cancer diagnosis?" (one of 14: Lung → Sarcoma) · "Has the cancer been staged? If spread, where?" · "Could you describe past and current treatments?" · consent to be contacted. Prep, thirty seconds, before every dial: read the form; write the diagnosis and the treatment line in the lead's own words; note the language (Spanish → the Spanish script, not an English rep improvising); note the time zone from the state. The call opens from the form, not from zero. A rep who asks "so what's the diagnosis?" on a form-lead dial has not read the form.

Fact sheet (ratified lines; every script reads from it)

  • Price: "Treatment starts at $35,000 per session. Plan on a minimum of one to two sessions; your doctor makes that decision based on his review of your file, and you'll know before you commit to anything."
  • Location: "Our administrative office is in California. All treatment is in Mexico, Los Cabos or Mexico City."
  • Insurance (v3 wording, panel note open): "We don't accept insurance and can't bill it. Families pay directly, and it's a no on insurance, not a no on getting here. Most families use some mix of savings, an unsecured personal loan from a US lender (several of them lend up to $100,000 and pay the money to you, not to us), family help, and sometimes a fundraiser. I'll email a one-page list of the options with the invite. We're not lenders or advisors, so the list points you to the lenders and the government resources, and if you want someone to walk through it with you, tell me and I'll set that up."
  • Review: "The case review is free. It needs your most recent scans and pathology. The medical team reviews it; then you speak with the physician."
  • FDA (only when asked; panel note open): "FDA status depends on the specific drugs, devices and uses the physician would propose. Treatment is offered only in Mexico. The physician identifies each component's status after review."
  • Outcomes, safety, timeframes: reps never state them.

Script 1: the first dial to a form lead (live answer) · the whole conversation

1"Hi, this is [name] from Williams Cancer Institute. Am I speaking with [first name]?" [wait] "You filled out the form on our site on [day] about [diagnosis, in their words]. This call is recorded for quality; is that okay?" [wait]

If no: "I've stopped the recording." Continue.

Wrong person / never filled out a form: apologize, remove the number, end.

Family member answers for the patient: "Are you the person who filled out the form for [patient first name]? Is [he/she] able to join, or are you the one I should be talking with?" Records and the medical conversation need the patient or someone with legal authority; a relative can hear everything else.

2"I'm sorry you're dealing with this. This usually takes twenty to thirty minutes if you have it now; if you don't, I'd rather book a time than rush it. Which is better?"

Now: continue at step 3.

Not now: "What day works, and is morning or afternoon better? … [day/time]. I'll send a calendar invite and my direct line. Have the most recent scan report and pathology report handy for the call; nothing to send yet." Then step 9 (the three facts), then close. On the booked call, Script 1 from step 3.

3"Your form says [diagnosis from the form], [staged / spread to X, in their words], and that you've had [treatments from the form]. Is that still where things stand, and what did the doctor say after the last scan?" Reflect once, in their words. No re-asking what the form already answered.

Caller states clear remission: "The team only reviews cases with an active tumor on recent imaging. If that changes, call me." End warmly.

4"The physician injects immunotherapy drugs directly into the tumor under image guidance, sometimes with cryoablation. Which drugs, and whether ablation is added, is the physician's call after reviewing the scans. I won't go further than that; I'm not the doctor."

5"Before I ask you to send anything: treatment starts at $35,000 per session. Plan on a minimum of one to two sessions; your doctor makes that decision based on his review of your file, and you'll know before you commit to anything. You'll have it in writing. We don't take insurance, and here's how families handle that." [insurance line from the fact sheet] "I'll stop there. What's your reaction?"

Cost is a hard stop: "Understood. I won't take records if cost is the wall. The review stays free if anything changes." End. No discount, no urgency.

"Does it work?": first, "I don't quote success rates; it depends on the type, the stage and the scans, and I'd be guessing. That's the physician's conversation after review." Second, "I understand why you're asking. I still won't put a number on it." Third, "I'm going to stop there; anything else would be me making it up. Let's get the records in and put that to the doctor."

6"If you want the team to look at the case, the review is free. It needs the most recent scans and pathology you have. I'm emailing the authorization and a secure upload link right now; I'll stay on while it lands. If the records are with the hospital, I'll send the release they need and you hand it to them; we can't pull records ourselves." The patient signs the authorization.

7"Once the packet is complete I call you when the team has looked. We're aiming for two business days, and I'll tell you if it's slipping. Let's put the packet check on the calendar now: [day/time]."

8"So: I've sent the authorization and the link, you send scans and pathology, I call you [day]. Did I get that right? If you decide this isn't for you, tell me; that's fine."

Empathy: three beats only (opening sentence, the reflection, the pause after money). The physician consultation is booked only after the review; the rep never previews outcomes.

9If the call is ending without the full conversation (not-now branch), say the three facts before hanging up so nobody books blind: "Three things so the call is worth your time: all treatment is in Mexico; we don't accept insurance, families pay directly and we'll talk through how; and treatment starts at $35,000 per session, plan on a minimum of one to two sessions, your doctor makes that decision from your file and you'll know before you commit to anything."

Cost is a hard stop: "Understood. The review stays free if that changes. I won't take more of your time today." End; no booking after a cost-no.

Script 2: the first dial reaches voicemail · twenty seconds

"Hi [first name], this is [name] from Williams Cancer Institute, calling about the form you sent on [day]. I'm emailing you my direct line, [number], and a short note on what happens next. Call me back when it suits you, or I'll try you again in two business days." No price, no procedure, no diagnosis, no Mexico on a machine. Log the dial; one more dial (Script 3) in two business days; then the email carries it.

Script 3: follow-up dial (email sent, no conversation yet) · two minutes, or the whole conversation

1"Hi [first name], [name] from Williams Cancer Institute; we sent you an email on [day] after your form about [diagnosis, in their words], and I wanted to make sure it reached you. Is now a bad time?"

Bad time: "What day works? … [day/time]." Book; end.

"I just wanted general information": "The email has our locations, the price line and what the review needs. If you'd like to talk it through, my direct line is in it." End.

"Stop calling": "Done. My direct line stays in the email if that changes." Mark do-not-dial; no third dial.

They have time now: Script 1 from step 3.

2Records already requested (the chase case): "The upload link is in the email from [day]; if the records are with the hospital, I'll resend the release for them. Do you want me to stay on the line while you find it?" Then Script 1 step 6.

Script 4: dial after an inbound call or voicemail · returning their call

"Hi [first name], [name] from Williams Cancer Institute, returning your call from [day]. Do you have twenty to thirty minutes now, or should we pick a time?" Now: Script 1 from step 1's consent line, then step 3 (there may be no form; ask the diagnosis question in full). Later: book; three facts; end.

Script 5: inbound, live

"Thanks for calling Williams Cancer Institute, this is [name]. This line can be recorded for quality; is that okay?" [wait] "I'm sorry you're dealing with this. If you have twenty to thirty minutes, I can walk through it properly now; if not, let's pick a time." Now: Script 1 from step 3 (no form: ask the diagnosis question in full). Later: book; three facts; end.

Spanish

13% of first dials are in Spanish, and the form's language field tells the rep before dialing. A Spanish-speaking rep runs the Spanish Script 1 and 2 (to be written from this draft once ratified); an English-only rep does not improvise in Spanish. Fact-sheet lines are translated once, by the clinic, and read verbatim.

Never say

success rate, percentage, "most patients" · guarantee, cure, remission prediction · "no side effects," "minimally invasive" as a safety answer · any pathology statistic · "FDA approved / cleared / registered" · stop, delay or replace current treatment · interpreting scans or pathology · "you qualify / you're a candidate" · "we treat all types / all stages" · any price below $35,000 or a course figure the doctor hasn't set · "insurance might cover / reimburse" · "financing" or "payment plan" as a WCI product · "reverse mortgage," "home equity," "borrow against" as a suggestion · a specific APR or monthly payment · "the consultation is virtual" as a promise · stay length or duration before ratification · pulling hospital records ourselves · drug brand names · urgency or scarcity · "I understand how you feel" · any unscheduled dial to a lead · the price band or the procedure on a call the caller cannot finish.

Test before rollout

Randomize by lead within rep, 8 weeks or sequential. Co-primaries: conversations held per 100 form leads (Script 1 from step 3 reached); written estimates issued per 100 leads. Gate: complaint, refund-request and attorney-contact rate non-inferior; price band or procedure left on a voicemail = 0; third dial after "stop calling" = 0; price-surprise callbacks; third-press outcome breaks. Report voicemail-reach rate and follow-up conversion separately (today: 35% of first dials hit a machine; 47% of follow-up dials become a conversation).
